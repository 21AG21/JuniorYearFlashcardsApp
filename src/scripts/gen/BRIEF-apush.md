# AP U.S. History — what the pilot (Period 1, 144 cards, 72 of 73 existing questions kept) settled

- Codes: `k` holds the key concept codes (KC-3.1.I.A form); the contextualizing and reasoning topics may use their period's summary codes (KC-3.1). `s` is a historical thinking skill (1.A to 6.D). Each topic has a thematic focus and a reasoning process (Comparison, Causation, Continuity and Change): its explain-why cards exercise that process.
- Answers: a historically defensible claim first, then specific evidence and the reasoning that connects them. The note names what earns nothing: description where the verb is explain; context that only restates the dates; a comparison that lists without naming the similarity or difference; a cause with no mechanism.
- The CED's illustrative examples and optional sources are not required: a card on a name or date the key concepts do not carry says in its note that it is illustrative. Estimates (population, casualties, votes) are flagged as estimates.
- FRQs in the May 2027 format (see SPEC.md's apush section): three required SAQs of 3 points (a secondary-source excerpt by a fictional historian; a primary-source excerpt written as a labelled paraphrase or composite; a non-text source described in words), one DBQ of 7 points with seven short documents, one LEQ of 6 points, both with the CED's rubric rows and a model plan by row. The DBQ is drawn from 1754 to 1980 on the exam; for Periods 2 and 9 open its stem with the "Practice." sentence SPEC.md gives.
- `b` marks a card that leans on another period. Illustrative real quotations stay short and well known; everything you compose is labelled.
