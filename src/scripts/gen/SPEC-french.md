# French: the adapted contract (draft — refined once the revised CED skeleton is in)

AP French is a proficiency framework, not a content framework: no EK codes, no unit weights, no exclusion statements of the science kind. The unit is a theme with recommended contexts and essential questions; the exam (May 2027, Bluebook) is Project Presentation 20 · Project Q&A 15 · Argumentative Essay 15 · Listening 25 · Reading 25.

Same output shape as SPEC.md, with these readings:
- `topics`: one per recommended context of the theme, in CED order, copied from the skeleton: `c` its slug code, `t` the context in French, `s` a skill code (1.B), `lo` and `ek` the learning objectives (every objective of the course is allowed).
- `k`: the learning-objective code(s) the card exercises; `s`: the skill code. Vocabulary: 2.A / 2.A.2. Cultural products, practices, perspectives: 3.A / 3.A.1 (describe), 3.A.2 (compare), 3.A.3 (reflect). Reading and listening: 1.A, 1.B, 1.C with their objectives. Connectors and organization: 2.D / 2.D.1, 2.D.2. Register: 2.A / 2.A.4. The unit has only 3 to 6 contexts, so each context carries 15 to 35 ordinary cards (80 to 140 for the unit in all).
- Ordinary cards (80–140): vocabulary in context (TRANSLATE, French → English with a French example sentence, as the existing cards do), cultural products/practices/perspectives of the Francophone world under the context (IDENTIFY, DESCRIBE, COMPARE), reading and listening moves (IDENTIFY the main idea, INTERPRET a tone or register), and presentational moves (STATE the connector, the structure). French with correct accents; answers in the language the task needs. Reuse existing vocabulary questions verbatim.
- Explain-why cards (40–50): in FRENCH (question and answer), the cultural comparisons and arguments the essay and the Q&A ask for: "Expliquez pourquoi …" with the chain in `h`, the model paragraph (≤ 320 chars) in `a`, other valid arguments in `o`, and weak answers in `w` (the answer as a student would write it, with why it earns little: no example, no connector, wrong register, a translation from English).
- Drill cards: a folded list of connectors, of register shifts (tu → vous), of a context's key terms.
- Cards never test grammar terminology or translation for its own sake (the exam does not).
- FRQ, five per theme: 1 `essay` (Argumentative Essay: a prompt on the theme, three sources you write and describe — a short article, a chart or table in words, a 60-second audio summarised — then `rows` from the essay's scoring dimensions as the skeleton gives them, and `parts` holding a model plan by dimension, in French), 1 `presentation` (a Project Presentation outline for a guiding question on the theme: `rows` from its scoring dimensions, `parts` = hook, context, two points with evidence, conclusion, in French), 1 `qa` (five likely Project Q&A questions on that presentation, each part's model 40-second answer in French, 3 moves: answer, reason with connector, example), 1 `mc` reading (a 200–300 word French text you write, 5 questions with the letter and reasoning), 1 `mc` listening (a transcript you write of about 150 words, 5 questions).

## Settled by the theme 1 pilot (follow these)
- The FRQ rows are the CED's scoring guidelines: Argumentative Essay 10 points, rows A Position 1 · B Source use 2 · C Argumentation 2 · D Organization 2 · E Language control 3. Project Presentation 11 points, rows A Task execution 2 · B Cultural understanding 2 · C Organization 2 · D Language control 3 · E Delivery 2. Project Q&A 9 points, rows A Task execution 2 · B Cultural understanding 2 · C Language control 3 · D Fluency 2. Read the input's scoring text for each row's descriptors.
- The presentation explains at least three factors (the CED minimum); the Q&A asks four questions, each heard twice, 40 seconds to answer, and at least one answer makes a cultural comparison. Reading has 30 questions and listening 25 on the exam; your two mc sets have five each.
- Every text, flyer, survey, chart and transcript you write is invented and labelled as such.
- Correct any existing note that is wrong (the pilot fixed aîné's circumflex, the school-zone calendar and la bise) and say so in the notes.
