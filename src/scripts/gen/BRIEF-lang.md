# AP English Language — what the pilot (Rhetorical Situation, 166 cards) settled

- The topics are the Big Idea's essential-knowledge statements in CED order (CLE-1.A …, REO-1.A …, STL-1.A …). Give each a short title of your own in `topics[].t` (the skeleton's titles are truncated EK text). `k` is the EK code, `s` the reading or writing skill from that EK's `lo` list (reading skill for a reading task, writing skill for a writing task).
- 3 to 8 ordinary cards per EK (80 to 140 for the Big Idea), 40 to 50 explain-whys in all. Question types: define the EK's term in the CED's words; identify it in a two-to-five-sentence passage you write, attributed to a described, invented speaker; explain how a choice serves purpose and audience; decide between two versions; write the sentence the writing skill asks for. Fold many-instance types into drills (fifteen excerpts, each labelled).
- Your `existing` list includes cards moved from Rhetorical Situation (tone and diction into Style, argument moves into Claims and Evidence). Reuse them verbatim where they fit an EK, like your own; term cards the CED never names may stay if they help, flagged in their notes.
- Verbs: IDENTIFY, DESCRIBE, EXPLAIN, ANALYZE, EVALUATE, WRITE, DEFINE, CONTRAST, DECIDE, STATE. No REVISE: produce a revision with WRITE, choose one with DECIDE.
- FRQs: rhetorical analysis, argument and synthesis, each with rows A Thesis 1 · B Evidence and commentary 4 · C Sophistication 1 tailored to the prompt, and parts holding a model plan by row; two mc sets of five four-choice questions on a 200–300 word passage you write (1 point each). Row A wording follows the CED rubric in the input; where you paraphrase the Row B or Row C descriptors, say so in the notes. The app shows no underlining, so a writing question quotes the portion it asks about.
- `b` marks a card that leans on another Big Idea (rhs, cle, reo, stl).
