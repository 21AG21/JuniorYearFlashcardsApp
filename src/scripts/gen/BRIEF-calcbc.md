# AP Calculus BC — what the pilot (Unit 1, 182 cards, all 55 existing questions kept) settled

- Justifications read like the scoring guidelines: name the theorem or test, state that its hypotheses hold (continuity on the closed interval, differentiability on the open one, the sign of f′ or f″ on both sides, the terms positive and decreasing), then conclude in words about the function in context. A trap note names the missing hypothesis, the wrong notation (a limit written as ∞ as if it existed; a derivative value given without its units; "the function is increasing because f′ is positive" without the interval), or the unlabelled units.
- Every figure is worked. Tables go one row per line (`x · f(x) · f′(x)`), graphs are described precisely enough to sketch (intercepts, extrema, inflection points, asymptotes, the values at the marked x).
- Mark each FRQ `calc` true or false. The long FRQs are 9 points in the exam's shapes, built from this unit's content (a unit rarely fills a whole real FRQ; a unit-only version of the shape is right). Short FRQs 4 to 5 points.
- A topic marked `bc: true` is BC-only: say so in the note of a card that would not appear on the AB exam. Topics with a `note` and no essential knowledge ("select a procedure") get selection drills tagged with the codes of the topics they draw on.
- The unit weight in the input is the CED's BC weighting; use it if you mention one.

Per-unit traps worth a card or a note:
- u2: the derivative defined as a limit of the difference quotient (both forms); differentiability implies continuity, not the converse (corner, cusp, vertical tangent); estimating f′ from a table with the symmetric or one-sided quotient and units.
- u3: the chain rule's missing inner derivative; implicit differentiation that forgets dy/dx on y terms; (f⁻¹)′(a) = 1/f′(f⁻¹(a)) evaluated at the wrong point; inverse-trig derivatives.
- u4: units of a rate in context; particle speeding up when v and a share a sign; related rates that substitute values before differentiating; local linearity over- or under-estimates by concavity; L'Hospital's Rule only for 0/0 or ∞/∞, with that form stated.
- u5: MVT and EVT hypotheses; the candidates test needs the endpoints; first- and second-derivative tests stated with the sign change; a point of inflection needs f″ to change sign; optimization answers that forget to justify the absolute extremum.
- u6: left, right, midpoint, trapezoid sums with unequal widths from a table and whether each over- or under-estimates; the FTC with a variable upper limit and the chain rule; accumulation g(x) = ∫ f from a graph (area above and below); integration by parts and partial fractions and improper integrals are BC.
- u7: slope fields drawn from the DE; separation of variables with the constant handled before solving for y and the domain; Euler's method steps as a table (BC); logistic dP/dt = kP(1 − P/L), the carrying capacity and the fastest growth at L/2 (BC).
- u8: average value; displacement against total distance; area with respect to y; washer volumes about lines other than the axes (radius as a distance); cross sections; arc length (BC).
- u9: dy/dx = (dy/dt)/(dx/dt) and the second derivative d/dt(dy/dx)/(dx/dt); speed and distance traveled for a vector-valued particle; polar area ½∫r² dθ and the region between two curves; dy/dx in polar.
- u10: every convergence test the CED names (nth-term, integral, comparison, limit comparison, alternating, ratio) with its conditions checked; absolute against conditional; the alternating series error bound and the Lagrange error bound; the interval of convergence with the endpoints tested separately; Maclaurin series of eˣ, sin x, cos x, 1/(1−x) and building new ones from them. The exclusion statement limits the tests to those six.
